# The definitive comparison of 7 major task management apps

**TickTick emerges as the most feature-complete all-in-one productivity app, while Todoist leads in natural language processing and ecosystem breadth, and Things 3 wins on design philosophy and the Apple experience.** The remaining four apps—Microsoft To Do, Google Tasks, Any.do, and Apple Reminders—each excel within their native ecosystems but trail the top two in standalone power. Below is an exhaustive feature-by-feature breakdown across all 10 categories, cross-cutting capabilities, and real user pain points.

---

## 1. Task creation and properties across all seven apps

**Todoist** offers the richest task property set among the subscription-based apps: titles with natural language parsing, Markdown descriptions (up to **16,383 characters**), **4 priority levels** (P1–P4, color-coded), unlimited labels (prefixed with @), due dates and times, a separate **Deadline field** (Pro only, added January 2025), **task duration** up to 24 hours (Pro only), deeply nested subtasks with full task properties, comments with file attachments (**5 MB free, 100 MB Pro**), and location-based reminders (Pro). Todoist lacks custom fields, energy levels, and native start dates—its most persistent user complaint.

**TickTick** nearly matches Todoist and exceeds it in several areas: **4 priority levels** (None/Low/Medium/High), tags with `#` syntax, due dates and times, **duration mode** with start/end times (Premium), subtasks up to **5 levels deep** (19 free / 199 Premium per task), comments, attachments (1 upload/day free, 99/day Premium), location-based reminders, task progress bars, and a unique **"Won't Do" status**. TickTick also lacks custom fields and energy levels but adds task-to-note conversion and batch editing.

**Things 3** takes a deliberately minimalist approach with a signature strength: **dual "When" (start) and "Deadline" (due) dates** on every task—the only app offering true start dates natively. Tasks have titles, Markdown-rich notes, tags (but **no priority levels by design**), checklists (lightweight subtasks without dates/tags), headings within projects, and URLs. Things notably **lacks file attachments, custom fields, location reminders, and comments**, trading feature breadth for focused simplicity.

**Microsoft To Do** provides titles, rich-text notes (web), due dates (no separate times—reminders carry time), **Steps** (subtasks with completion counter), **binary importance** (Normal or Important—only 2 levels), file attachments up to **25 MB**, hashtag tags in task names, and Outlook categories. No custom fields, no duration, no location reminders.

**Google Tasks** is the most stripped-down: titles, a details field (~8,100 characters), due dates and times, **one level of subtasks only**, stars for importance, and task duration (added 2025 for calendar time-blocking). It **completely lacks** priority levels, tags/labels, attachments, comments, custom fields, and location reminders.

**Any.do** offers titles with NLP parsing, notes, due dates/times, color tags (Premium), unlimited subtasks, attachments from cloud storage (Premium), location-based reminders (Premium, mobile), and **custom fields** (Teams/Workspace only). Its AI can suggest subtasks for complex tasks.

**Apple Reminders** has grown substantially: titles, rich-text notes (iOS 16+), dedicated URL field, due dates/times with **per-reminder time zone support** (iOS 26), **4 priority levels**, hashtag tags, unlimited nested subtasks, photo/scan attachments, location reminders (arrive/leave/car Bluetooth), unique **"When Messaging" reminders**, flags, and the new **Urgent alarm-style alerts** (iOS 26.2). No custom fields.

## 2. How reminders and notifications compare

The reminder system is where these apps diverge most sharply. **TickTick's "Constant Reminder"** is the standout: notifications **ping every minute** until you Dismiss, Snooze, mark Done, or Start a Focus session. This can be enabled globally or per-task (mobile only) and is the only true persistent/nagging reminder among all seven apps. TickTick also offers **2 reminders per task (free) or 5 (Premium)**, priority-based custom ringtones, end-time reminders for duration tasks, daily digest notifications, and lock screen pinning on iOS.

**Apple Reminders** recently closed the gap with **Urgent reminders** (iOS 26.2)—alarm-style alerts that sound even when the device is silenced or Focus is active, with snooze (~10 minutes) and slide-to-stop controls. Apple also uniquely offers **Early Reminders** (iOS 17+) with presets from 5 minutes to 1 month before due, plus location-based and "When Messaging" triggers. Standard reminders fire once and are not persistent.

**Todoist** auto-reminds 30 minutes before any timed task (customizable). **Custom reminders are Pro-only** and support multiple per task, including date/time, relative ("1 hour before"), recurring, and location-based. No persistent/nagging option exists. Snooze intervals are customizable in settings.

**Microsoft To Do** allows **one reminder per task** with presets (Later Today, Tomorrow, Next Week, custom). No persistent reminders. Snoozing is limited—you must manually edit the reminder time after it fires. Notifications sync across Windows, iOS, and Android.

**Google Tasks** requires both a date AND time to trigger any notification. No snooze button exists for task notifications. Google's official documentation mentions **"nudges" that continue prompting until a task is completed**, and overdue tasks persist in Calendar's all-day section—the closest Google comes to nagging.

**Any.do** offers one reminder per personal task (time-based OR location-based, not both). Board tasks (Family/Teams) support **multiple reminders** with preset intervals. Snooze options: 15 minutes, 1 hour, or Tomorrow. WhatsApp reminder delivery is a unique Premium feature.

**Things 3** is the most limited: **one reminder per task**, tied to the When date's time. No persistent reminders. Snooze intervals of **10, 30, or 60 minutes**. Deadlines cannot have separate reminders.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reminders per task | Multiple (Pro) | 2 free / 5 Premium | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 (personal) / Multiple (boards) | 1 + Early Reminder |
| Persistent/nagging | ❌ | ✅ (Constant Reminder) | ❌ | ❌ | Partial (nudges) | ❌ | ✅ (Urgent, iOS 26.2) |
| Location-based | ✅ (Pro) | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ (Premium) | ✅ |
| Snooze | ✅ (custom) | ✅ (custom date/time) | 10/30/60 min | Limited | ❌ | 15 min/1 hr/Tomorrow | ✅ (Urgent: ~10 min) |

## 3. Recurring task flexibility varies enormously

**Todoist** has the most expressive recurrence engine, driven by natural language. It parses complex patterns like **"every 2nd Tuesday," "every last weekday of the month," "every other Wednesday starting Jan 15 ending March 10."** The critical `every` vs. `every!` distinction handles fixed-schedule vs. after-completion recurrence. Subtasks can reset automatically when a recurring parent is completed. Maximum date expression length is 150 characters.

**TickTick** covers standard patterns (daily/weekly/monthly/yearly/custom intervals) with both **"Due Date" and "Completion Date" repeat types**. It supports skip-one-recurrence, editing single occurrences in calendar view, and setting end conditions (specific date or X repetitions). Complex patterns like "every 2nd Tuesday" are not natively supported.

**Apple Reminders** is surprisingly powerful: hourly, daily, weekdays, weekends, weekly, biweekly, monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, and yearly presets, plus fully custom options selecting specific weekdays, days of month, or **pattern-based rules** ("last weekday of each month," "third Tuesday"). End-repeat dates supported.

**Things 3** offers fixed-schedule and after-completion modes with daily/weekly/monthly/yearly intervals, specific weekday selection, and end dates. Entire **projects can repeat** (not just tasks). The limitation: you cannot complete a repeating task early before its scheduled appearance.

**Microsoft To Do** handles daily, weekdays, weekly, monthly, yearly, and custom intervals with specific weekdays. Complex patterns like "3rd Wednesday of every month" **require creation in Outlook** and syncing back. No "regenerate after completion" in To Do itself. Subtask completion states do not reset on recurrence.

**Any.do** restricts advanced recurring (custom intervals, specific days, end dates) to **Premium only**. Board-level recurring tasks offer trigger-based recurrence (on archive, when moved to section, or when tagged).

**Google Tasks** supports basic daily/weekly/monthly/yearly recurrence with custom frequency. Lacks "after completion" mode, cannot handle "first Monday of month" patterns, and offers no skip/postpone for individual occurrences without going through Google Calendar.

## 4. Organizational structures and hierarchy

The depth of project organization differs substantially:

**Todoist** provides the most structured hierarchy for power users: **nested sub-projects** (multiple levels), **sections within projects** (up to 20), labels, and an Inbox. Free users get **5 projects**; Pro gets **300**; Business gets up to **500 team projects** plus **1,000 folders**. Filters use Boolean query syntax (with AI-powered Filter Assist). Projects and labels can be favorited and color-coded.

**TickTick** organizes as **Folders → Lists → Sections → Tasks → Subtasks** (5 levels). Free: **9 lists**; Premium: **299**. Sections double as Kanban columns. Tags provide cross-list categorization. Custom smart lists (Premium) filter across the whole system. A **"Plan Your Day"** feature walks through overdue/today tasks one by one.

**Things 3** implements a GTD-inspired hierarchy: **Areas → Projects → Headings → To-Dos → Checklists**. Areas represent life categories, projects are goal-oriented collections, and headings serve as section dividers within projects. The **Inbox → Today → Upcoming → Anytime → Someday → Logbook** flow is fixed but elegant. Tags provide cross-cutting filtering. Projects cannot nest within projects, and areas cannot nest within areas.

**Apple Reminders** offers lists with custom **colors and SF Symbol icons**, list groups (folders), **sections within lists** (iOS 17+), tags, and both built-in and custom Smart Lists with multi-criteria filtering. **Templates** (iOS 16+) allow saving and sharing list structures.

**Microsoft To Do** uses Lists organized into **Groups** (collapsible folders). Built-in smart lists include My Day, Important, Planned, Flagged Emails, and Assigned to Me. No custom smart lists or saved filters exist.

**Any.do** organizes through personal Lists and collaborative Boards (Family/Teams). Boards have customizable sections/columns. **100+ workflow templates** exist for Teams/Workspace boards.

**Google Tasks** is flattest: unlimited lists with no folders, no sections, no tags, no smart lists, and no filters. Tasks can be starred. Sorting is limited to manual order or by date.

## 5. Available views determine daily workflow

**TickTick** offers the most views: **List, Kanban (Premium), Timeline (Premium), 5 Calendar views (Month/Week/Day/3-Day/Agenda—Premium for most), Eisenhower Matrix (all plans)**, Today/Tomorrow/Next 7 Days smart views, and desktop Sticky Notes.

**Any.do** provides surprising breadth at the Teams tier: List, Calendar (free), My Day, **Kanban (Family+), Table view (Teams), and Gantt chart (Teams)**. Mobile board views are limited to table-like display.

**Todoist** offers **List (free), Board/Kanban (free), and Calendar (Pro only)** in day/week/month views. No Gantt/timeline view exists natively.

**Apple Reminders** includes Today, Scheduled, Flagged, All, Completed, custom Smart Lists, **Column/Kanban view** (iOS 17+ for sectioned lists), and integration with the Calendar app (iOS 18+) where reminders appear alongside events.

**Things 3** has fixed views: Inbox, Today (with optional "This Evening"), Upcoming (timeline-style), Anytime, Someday, and Logbook. **No calendar view, no Kanban, no custom views.** Multiple windows on Mac/iPad partially compensate.

**Microsoft To Do** offers My Day, Planned, Important, All, and individual list views. **No calendar view, no Kanban, no weekly planner** within To Do itself (these exist in Microsoft Planner as a separate product).

**Google Tasks** is the most limited: list view in the app, Gmail/Workspace sidebar panel, and a **fullscreen view within Google Calendar** (where time-blocking was added in November 2025). No standalone calendar, Kanban, or smart views.

## 6. Integration ecosystems and third-party connections

**Todoist** leads with **90+ integrations**: two-way Google Calendar and Outlook sync, Gmail/Outlook add-ons, full REST and Sync APIs, native Slack/Teams/IFTTT/Zapier support, browser extensions for Chrome/Firefox/Safari/Edge, Siri/Google Assistant/Alexa, and the AI-powered **Ramble** voice-to-task feature. Widgets span iOS, Android, and desktop.

**TickTick** offers two-way Google Calendar sync (with a frustrating **~15-minute delay**), calendar subscriptions (Premium), an open API, IFTTT/Zapier/Make support, Siri/Google Assistant, browser extensions for Chrome/Firefox/Safari, and email-to-task. It integrates with Spark email and supports Apple Shortcuts.

**Microsoft To Do** gains its power from the **Microsoft 365 ecosystem**: deep bidirectional Outlook sync, flagged-email-to-task conversion, Planner integration in Teams, Power Automate flows, and the Microsoft Graph API. IFTTT and Zapier are supported. Voice assistant integration has weakened since Cortana's deprecation.

**Google Tasks** lives inside Google's ecosystem: Gmail email-to-task, Calendar integration with **time-blocking** (November 2025), Google Chat task assignment, Docs/Sheets task creation, **Gemini AI** natural language management, and the Tasks API (50,000 queries/day). Third-party tools like TasksBoard extend functionality.

**Apple Reminders** integrates deeply within Apple's ecosystem: Siri, Shortcuts, Calendar app (iOS 18+), Safari, Mail, Maps, Phone ("Call Back Reminders" in iOS 26), Messages, Apple Intelligence, widgets, and Control Center. Third-party access via EventKit API. **No CalDAV sync** for modern upgraded reminders.

**Things 3** compensates for its Apple-only ecosystem with an **extensive URL scheme**, full AppleScript support (Mac), comprehensive Apple Shortcuts actions, Mail to Things (email-to-task), and Quick Entry with Autofill from Safari/Mail/Finder. No REST API exists.

**Any.do** differentiates with **WhatsApp integration** (create tasks and receive reminders via WhatsApp), plus calendar sync with Google/Outlook/Apple/Facebook Events, Gmail integration, Zapier/Slack/Jira connections, and browser extensions for Chrome/Firefox/Edge/Safari.

## 7. Collaboration ranges from team-grade to nonexistent

**Todoist** offers the strongest collaboration: shared projects (up to **250 people** in Business), single-assignee task assignment, threaded comments with attachments and @mentions, unlimited activity history (Pro+), team workspaces with admin/member/guest roles, and shared templates. Business plan includes centralized billing and SOC2 certification.

**Any.do** provides solid team features at the Workspace tier: unlimited boards, unlimited members, task assignment (multiple assignees), in-task chat, admin tools with role-based access, guest access via read-only links, and **100+ workflow templates**.

**Microsoft To Do** supports shared lists with equal-edit access for all members, basic task assignment (work/school accounts), and Planner integration for richer team workflows. Limitations include no comments, no activity log, and no role-based permissions within To Do itself.

**TickTick** allows shared lists with task assignment (**1 collaborator free, 29 Premium**), comments, @mentions, and task activity history (Premium). Collaboration is functional but widely described as "an afterthought."

**Apple Reminders** enables iCloud list sharing with real-time sync and task assignment to participants (iOS 16+). Notification controls for shared lists are granular. Limitations: must use iCloud, can't share Smart Lists, no comments.

**Google Tasks** has **no shared task lists**. Workarounds exist through Google Chat Spaces (task assignment within Spaces) and Google Docs (task assignment via checklists), but no native collaborative task management.

**Things 3** is **explicitly personal-only**—zero collaboration features by design. No shared projects, no task assignment, no comments, no team features.

## 8. Gamification and motivation features

**TickTick** provides the richest motivation system: an **Achievement Score** with 10 badge levels (unlocking exclusive themes), weekly/monthly completion statistics, best working day analysis, habit streaks with check-in calendars, Pomodoro focus statistics (time per task, most productive hours), and a comparison metric showing how productive you are relative to other users.

**Todoist** runs the well-known **Karma system**: points for completing tasks on time, 8 levels from Beginner to Enlightened, daily and weekly goal tracking, completion streaks with break days and vacation mode, and productivity visualizations (color-coded bar charts by project). Karma can be disabled entirely.

**Microsoft To Do, Google Tasks, Things 3, and Apple Reminders** offer **no gamification**. Microsoft To Do's satisfying completion sound is its closest motivational element. Things 3's Logbook serves as a completionist's archive but without analytics.

**Any.do** has minimal gamification—a basic streak feature and the **Moment** daily planning ritual, but no points, badges, or productivity dashboards.

## 9 cross-cutting features and which apps support them

### Persistent/nagging notifications
**TickTick** is the clear leader with its **Constant Reminder** that pings every minute until acted on (mobile only). **Apple Reminders** added **Urgent reminders** in iOS 26.2 that function as alarms—sounding even when silenced. **Google Tasks** has rudimentary "nudges" that re-surface overdue tasks. Todoist, Things 3, Microsoft To Do, and Any.do **do not offer** persistent reminders.

### Built-in Pomodoro timer
**TickTick only.** It provides a full-featured system: 25-minute Pomo countdown or open-ended stopwatch, customizable intervals, task association for time tracking, **17+ white noise/ambient sounds**, Strict Mode (limits phone use), Flip Start (phone must be face-down), focus statistics, and sync across devices. All other apps require third-party integrations (Todoist → PomoDone; others → various timer apps).

### Habit tracking
**TickTick only.** A dedicated module with **60+ pre-built habits**, custom habits, flexible frequency (daily/X times per week/specific days), quantified goals, streak tracking, monthly check-in calendar, Heat Map widget, and per-habit reminders. No other app in this comparison has built-in habit tracking.

### Eisenhower matrix view
**TickTick only.** A core module mapping tasks to four quadrants using two default rule sets: priority-only or priority + time urgency. Fully customizable quadrant rules, drag-and-drop between quadrants, filterable by list/tag, and available as a widget. Available on both free and Premium plans.

### Natural language task input
**Todoist** is best-in-class—parsing dates, times, recurrence, priorities (#project, @label, +assignee, p1–p4) in **15+ languages** from a single text input. **TickTick** parses dates and times but handles complex patterns less reliably. **Things 3** parses dates/times in the When and Deadline pickers across 8 languages. **Microsoft To Do** (Windows/iOS) recognizes dates, times, and basic recurrence in task titles (English only). **Any.do** parses dates and times with NLP. **Apple Reminders** relies on Siri for natural language, which handles dates, times, locations, and recurrence well. **Google Tasks** has no native NLP—natural language requires Gemini AI.

### Quick add from anywhere
**Todoist**: Global shortcut Ctrl+Q (Windows) / Option+Q (Mac) opens Quick Add overlay system-wide. **Things 3**: Quick Entry with Autofill (Ctrl+Space on Mac) grabs context from the active app. **TickTick**: Global desktop shortcut. **Microsoft To Do**: No global shortcut; quick add within the app. **Google Tasks**: No global shortcut. **Any.do**: No global shortcut; widget-based. **Apple Reminders**: Spotlight integration on macOS 26+ ("New Reminder") and Control Center.

### Focus / do not disturb modes
**TickTick** offers Pomodoro-integrated focus features: Strict Mode, app Allowlisting, Flip Start, Full-Screen Mode, and per-shared-list notification muting. **Any.do** references a Focus Mode in its app store listing, integrated with My Day. **Google Tasks** time-blocking (November 2025) enables DND and auto-declines meetings during task blocks. Others rely on OS-level DND.

### Time blocking and calendar integration
**Todoist** (Pro) enables visual time-blocking: drag tasks to calendar time slots, set durations, see tasks alongside Google/Outlook calendar events. **TickTick** (Premium) provides similar capabilities with duration tasks, drag-and-drop calendar scheduling, and unified task+event views. **Google Tasks** added calendar time-blocking in November 2025 (mark busy, enable DND, auto-decline). **Any.do** offers calendar integration and Gantt views (Teams tier). **Microsoft To Do** tasks appear as Outlook reminders but **cannot be placed as calendar blocks** natively—third-party tools like Morgen bridge this gap. Things 3 and Apple Reminders show calendar events but lack time-blocking.

### Task templates
**Todoist** offers **project templates** (100 per account, 50+ pre-built, shared templates in Business). **TickTick** supports saving individual tasks as templates. **Apple Reminders** allows saving lists as templates (iOS 16+), shareable via iCloud links. **Any.do** provides **100+ workflow templates** for Teams boards. **Things 3** uses repeating projects as de facto templates. **Microsoft To Do** and **Google Tasks** have no template functionality.

## What Todoist users complain about most

The most persistent frustration is the **absence of true start dates**. Despite years of requests, Todoist still cannot hide tasks until a designated start date—the January 2025 Deadline field inverts the concept but doesn't solve it. Users who follow GTD methodology frequently cite this as their reason for switching to Things 3 or TickTick.

**No task dependencies** ranks second. Tasks cannot block or trigger other tasks, making Todoist inadequate for complex project management. A Hacker News commenter captured the sentiment: "It seems so strange to do all the hard work of nested projects and complex filters, but then not do dependencies."

The **calendar view** was added in 2024 after nearly a decade of requests, but users still find it "half-baked"—it's **Pro-only**, connects to only one calendar provider at a time, and external events are read-only. **Reminders remaining a paid feature** (custom reminders require Pro) is a frequent complaint, as is the restrictive free tier of just 5 projects and 3 filters. Filter syntax complexity, no Gantt/timeline view, no built-in Pomodoro timer, and the **December 2025 price increase** (Pro now $48/year, up from prior pricing) round out the top complaints. The controversial "Pro Legacy" plan—freezing features for long-time subscribers unless they pay more—has generated significant backlash.

## What TickTick users complain about most

The **~15-minute calendar sync delay** with Google Calendar is the most technically frustrating issue. External calendar events don't update in real-time, breaking workflows for users who depend on cross-app coordination.

**Dated UI design** is the most common aesthetic criticism. Reviewers consistently describe TickTick's interface as "cluttered" and "old-fashioned" compared to Things 3's elegance or Todoist's minimalism—a side effect of cramming so many features into one app.

The **free tier's restrictions** frustrate users who discover that TickTick's best feature—the calendar view—is paywalled. Other Premium-only features include Kanban boards, custom smart lists, calendar subscriptions, and detailed statistics. **Collaboration is widely seen as an afterthought**: limited to basic shared lists with no real-time commenting threads, team workspaces, or robust permissions. **Natural language parsing trails Todoist significantly**, especially for complex recurring patterns. The **API is underdeveloped** (no webhooks, limited endpoints, sparse documentation). **Privacy concerns** about TickTick's Beijing-based parent company persist, though data is hosted on US-based AWS servers with GDPR compliance. **Notification reliability** across devices is inconsistent, and the Constant Reminder interval (every minute) is not configurable. Sorting and filtering hit a ceiling at **3 advanced filter conditions maximum**.

Users most frequently request **automatic task rollover** (per-task configuration), **"do date"/start date** separate from due date, **notes fields for habits**, **table support in descriptions**, **more than 3 filter conditions**, **customizable keyboard shortcuts**, and a **full-featured open API with webhooks**.

## The bottom line for choosing the right app

These seven apps occupy distinct niches rather than competing head-to-head. **TickTick** delivers the most features per dollar—a genuine all-in-one tool with Pomodoro, habits, calendar, Eisenhower matrix, and nagging reminders, ideal for individual power users who want everything in one app. **Todoist** excels for users who prioritize natural language input, a polished API ecosystem, and team collaboration. **Things 3** is the premium choice for Apple-only users who value design clarity, the When/Deadline dual-date system, and freedom from subscriptions. **Microsoft To Do** is the obvious pick for anyone embedded in Microsoft 365, especially with Outlook email-to-task and Planner integration. **Google Tasks** suits Google Workspace users who want simple task management integrated into Gmail and Calendar without adding another app. **Any.do** distinguishes itself with WhatsApp integration, AI features, and a Gantt view at the Teams tier. **Apple Reminders** has evolved from a basic utility into a capable task manager with unique features—location/messaging triggers, Urgent alarm reminders, grocery auto-categorization, and Column view—all completely free.

The gap between these apps is narrowing rapidly. Todoist's 2024–2025 calendar and deadline additions, Apple Reminders' iOS 17–26 feature explosion, and Google Tasks' time-blocking update all demonstrate how competitive pressure is pushing every app forward. The most important remaining differentiators are TickTick's all-in-one approach versus Todoist's ecosystem breadth versus Things 3's design philosophy—and whether you need collaboration, which eliminates Things 3 and Google Tasks immediately.
